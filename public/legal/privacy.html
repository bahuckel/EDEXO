What stays on your computer

ED Exo Compare reads the journal files Elite Dangerous writes on your computer and works on them there: merging scans, listing candidate species, estimating values. It never changes your journal files.

Its own data — settings, the species you scanned on foot, surface marks, statistics, the miss log, looked-up systems, your HUD layout — lives in its data folder (%LOCALAPPDATA%\ED Exo Compare on Windows, ~/.config/edexo-compare on Linux). Backups are written only to the folder you choose and are never sent anywhere. The exomastery and codex downloads are saved only where you save them.

There are no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reports and no advertising, and no personal data is sold or shared for marketing.

What leaves your computer

Every service is named here, with when and what, so you can read what leaves your machine without running it. Any request also shows that service your IP address, as every internet request does.

Always, with no setting

  • Update check — GitHub (api.github.com). When the launcher opens, at most once an hour (and when you press Check for updates), the app asks GitHub for the list of this project's releases. It sends nothing about you; the app's version is in the request's User-Agent. If a newer version exists, the launcher downloads it from this project's GitHub releases only when you press Download & Install, checks it against the checksum GitHub publishes, and installs it by restarting the app. Your settings and data stay where they are. The release page stays one click away. Builds that cannot replace themselves (run from source, the console builds) open the release page instead.
  • Game server status — ed-server-status.orerve.net, and EDSM if that does not answer. While the app's window is open, about once a minute, to show whether the game's servers are up. Nothing about you is sent.
  • Route check — EDSM (www.edsm.net). When you have a route plotted, the names of the systems on it (up to 40 per request, at least 5 seconds apart) are sent to EDSM to see which it already knows — how the app tells whether a first footfall may still be possible. Only the system names are sent: never your commander name or your EDSM key.
  • Community Goals — Frontier (api.orerve.net). About 30 seconds after the app starts and then once an hour, the app asks Frontier's public list of running Community Goals — the list Frontier's own website reads — to give you a notice when a new one starts. It sends nothing about you; the app's version is in the request's User-Agent. Switch it off in Options → Notify me → Community goals and the request stops.

When you do something

  • System search — Spansh (spansh.co.uk). Typing three or more letters in the system search sends what you typed. Opening a result for a system you have not scanned yourself fetches that system's data by its number; it is kept on your computer for 30 days. The carrier check in the Carriers screen sends a carrier's callsign when you press it.
  • Carriers and points of interest — EDAstro (edastro.com). The download buttons in the Carriers and Points of Interest screens fetch EDAstro's public lists. Nothing about you is sent.
  • Your public address — ipify (api.ipify.org). Only when you click the eye icon beside Public internet in the launcher's network settings, to show the address a device outside your home network would use. The address is kept on your computer; Forget deletes it.
  • NavRoute check — EDSM (www.edsm.net). When you press Check EDSM in the galaxy map's NavRoute drawer, the names of the systems listed (up to 40 per request, at least 5 seconds apart) are sent to EDSM to see which it knows. Only the system names are sent.
  • Galaxy index — GitHub (github.com). When you press Download in the galaxy map, its index (about 255 MB) is downloaded from this project's GitHub releases. Nothing about you is sent.

Only if you switch it on (all off by default)

  • Look up systems when I jump — EDSM. Needs your EDSM API key. As you jump, the name of a system you have no scans of is sent to EDSM with your EDSM commander name and key, so the app can show what is there.
  • Send my journal to EDSM. Needs your EDSM API key. Your journal events (without the kinds EDSM asks clients to leave out), with your EDSM commander name, key and the game's version, are sent to EDSM — when you press Catch up, or every few minutes if you choose Keep sending as I play. The app remembers what it has sent so nothing is sent twice.
  • Send my discoveries to Canonn (Canonn's research API). While you play, organic scans, sales of organic data, codex entries and the other events Canonn asks for are sent as the full journal line, with your commander name, the system's name and coordinates, and the app's version.
  • Send my live events to EDDN (eddn.edcd.io, the community data network). While you play: jumps, locations, docking, body scans and signals, organic scans (log and sample), codex entries, and your plotted route, with personal fields such as fuel and fines removed and, for organic scans and codex entries, the position on the surface. EDDN's uploader field carries your commander name. EDDN passes this on publicly to anyone listening.

Your use of these services is also subject to their own terms and privacy policies — for example EDSM’s privacy policy and GitHub’s privacy statement.

The EDEXO browser

The desktop app's Browser tab is a small private browser. What it does with your data:

  • Nothing is collected. No history is kept, nothing about the pages you open is sent to us or anyone else, and there is no telemetry. Pages are fetched from the sites you visit, as in any browser; every request carries Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control, and the browser names itself as plain Chrome (no app name).
  • Private by default. What a site stores (cookies, local storage, cache) is cleared when the app closes and again when the browser first opens, unless you press Remember this site for it: then its cookies and logins are kept between starts, and nothing else is. Forget this site and Clear everything now remove them at once. Which sites you remember, where blocking is off, your start-page links and your search engine are kept in edexo-browser.json in the app's data folder; the browser's own data is in its own session there, apart from the app's.
  • Keep page cache (off by default): when you switch it on, the browser's page cache (copies of pages, scripts and images it fetched) is kept between starts so sites you visited load faster; cookies and site storage are still cleared as above. The cache stays on this PC and can show which sites you visited; Clear everything now empties it.
  • Remember passwords (off by default): when you switch it on, the browser offers to save the username and password you sign in with on an HTTPS site, and fills them in again on that same site only: on its own when one login is saved for it, or with the key button when there are several. They are encrypted with your operating system's protection (Windows' data protection, the macOS keychain or a Linux keyring, through Electron's safeStorage) before they are written to browser/passwords.json in the app's data folder; where no such protection exists nothing is saved. They are kept only on this PC and never sent anywhere. You can delete them one by one or all at once in the browser's menu; switching the option off keeps them but stops saving and filling. An optional master password adds a second lock: the logins are then also encrypted with a key made from it (scrypt, AES-256-GCM), which is kept in memory only from the moment you unlock until the app closes or you lock it. The master password itself is never stored. Saved passwords are left out of the app's backups.
  • Ads and trackers are blocked on every site unless you switch blocking off for one. The blocklists are downloaded only when you press Download the blocklists the first time, then again in the background, from their publishers: EasyList and EasyPrivacy (easylist.to) and uBlock Origin's lists (ublockorigin.github.io) once a week; uBlock's quick fixes daily, with uBlock's scriptlet library as Ghostery packages it (raw.githubusercontent.com, Ghostery's adblocker repository). The scriptlets are small scripts the lists run on some sites (YouTube among them) before the site's own code, to take the ads out of what the site is sent; they run only on the site they are for, and send nothing anywhere. Those downloads show those servers your IP address and send nothing else.
  • Searches typed in the address bar go to the search engine you chose (DuckDuckGo unless you change it) and are subject to its privacy policy.
  • Downloads ask where to save every time and are never opened by the app. No form autofill beyond Remember passwords, no extensions. Sites get no camera, microphone, location, notifications or any other permission.

Your keys

Your EDSM API key is kept in its own file in the app's data folder, never in the settings file, and is only ever sent to edsm.net over HTTPS. You can delete it in Options at any time, which also switches off everything that needs it. The LAN key protects the app's pages when you open them to other devices in Network settings; it stays on your computer. Neither key goes into a backup unless you tick Your EDSM and LAN keys.

Links

Links in the app (to EDSM, EDAstro, Canonn, GitHub, photo credits and others) open in your web browser and are subject to those sites’ own policies; so are the sites you open in the EDEXO browser.
